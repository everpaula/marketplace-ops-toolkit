Quarterly review. A review-ops lead at a high-growth ecommerce platform had one line flagged: 60% of case review running on vendor seats, a per-case rate locked three years ago. Vendor looked like the cheap option.

36 vendor reviewers, 24 in-house, a rate card nobody had reopened since signing. The comparison everyone trusted was day-rate against day-rate. Vendor looked cheaper per seat, so the split kept drifting.

Day-rate math hid the real number. Once productivity and shrinkage went in honestly, vendor cost landed near $4.20 per case against $3.10 in-house. The drift had added $360K a year on the worse option.

She ran three models on the same inputs: full in-house, her 60-40 hybrid, full vendor. Volume by case type, handle time, productive minutes, shrinkage, peak ratio. Cost per case, not cost per seat.

Hybrid still won, but not at 60% vendor. Shifting 12 cases a day back in-house and cutting the vendor pool to a smaller flex tier recovered about $230K in year one, peak coverage intact.

The cheap vendor was the expensive one. A lower day rate that needs more seats to clear the same volume costs more per case than the team it was meant to undercut.

Seat cost is what you negotiate. Case cost is what you pay.
