Q4 forecast hit a regional 3PL on a Tuesday. 35% volume jump over last year. Linear model said add 50 couriers, POs by Friday. $180K of wrong-sizing risk either way.

140 couriers. 18 vans. 3 hubs. The spreadsheet she had used three Q4 cycles in a row said the same thing: bottleneck is people.

Old playbook scaled couriers with volume, fleet stayed flat. Worked at 15% Q4 jumps. At 35%, Day 3 overflow saturated the vans. Couriers idle. OT bleeding to clear before Day 4 inbound landed.

She rebuilt the model two-layer: processing capacity and storage as independent constraints. Day-over-day cascade made today's overflow tomorrow's inbound. Couriers hit 78%. Vans hit 96%. The bottleneck was the fleet, not the labor.

8 extra vehicles. 22 couriers, not 50. Q4 ran at 94% on-time vs 89% prior. Net cost $140K under the linear-model plan.

The forecast was right. The model was wrong about which resource hit the wall. Adding 50 couriers when 18 vans are the choke point buys you a more expensive version of the same problem.

Linear models tell you what's missing. Cascade models tell you what's already broken upstream.
