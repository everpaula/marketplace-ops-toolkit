About this calculator

Every operations leader running a manual review queue eventually faces the same question: which queues should we scale, and which are losing us money? Most teams answer it with intuition. The math to answer it properly isn't complex — what's useful is having a structure to do it consistently, every quarter, across markets and case types.

This calculator codifies that structure. It's generalized from years of running fraud and marketplace operations across multiple geographies in Latin America. Open-source for any ops leader making the same call.

The formula

Agent ROI = (Orders reviewed × Accuracy × Avg savings/cancellation) ÷ Daily cost

Tier classification

ROI ≥ 1.0 — Scale Each $1 in agent cost generates $1+ in savings. Add headcount, expand case mix, raise the bar on what flows to the queue.
0.5 ≤ ROI < 1.0 — Optimize Marginal. Don't scale yet. Tune one lever before adding people. Usually accuracy or orders/agent.
ROI < 0.5 — Root cause Negative. Adding more agents amplifies the loss. Investigate rule precision, tooling, training, or whether this case type belongs in a queue at all.

Use cases

  • Fraud operations queue management (the original use case)
  • Content moderation teams reviewing flagged content
  • Customer service quality assurance review
  • KYC and compliance manual review
  • Any operations function with a manual decision queue and measurable savings per intervention

What this calculator does NOT replace

  • Forecasting volume changes — you still need a separate model for that
  • Vendor vs in-house split decisions — ROI calculation is one input, not the whole call
  • Quality framework design (sampling, calibration, IRR) — different problem
  • Rule precision tuning — that's where Fraud Strategy lives, not Operations

A note on the benchmarks

The decomposition bars compare your inputs against rough industry averages. They are not a competitive benchmark — they are a sanity check. If your orders-per-agent is 50 against a 250 benchmark, that's a signal to investigate tooling or process bottlenecks, not a verdict that your team is bad. Real benchmarks come from your own data over time.
