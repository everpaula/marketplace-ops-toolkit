What could move the number

  • Exemption annexes. The forced-labor notice exempts products listed in Annex I and Annex II, Part A. In the Federal Register HTML those tables are images, and this report did not confirm line by line that 3918.10 is outside them. Ask your broker which chapter 99 line goes on your entry.
  • A cut to 10%. USTR can lower the 12.5% rate to 10% for a country that adopts a forced-labor import ban USTR finds could work. Vietnam issued Decree 292/2026/ND-CP on July 22, 2026. No notice lowering Vietnam's rate was found as of November 16, 2026. Medium Kelley Drye
  • A second Section 301 case on Vietnam. USTR opened an investigation into Vietnam's intellectual property practices on May 29, 2026. Trade press lists the determination as due around November 29, 2026, and tariffs are among the possible actions. Medium C.H. Robinson, Jun 8, 2026; Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg
  • Court challenge. An importers' group sued at the Court of International Trade on July 24, 2026, arguing the forced-labor tariffs fall outside Section 301 authority. Medium Holland & Knight

Antidumping, countervailing duty and evasion cases

These cases are general background on how US agencies enforce trade and origin rules for products from Vietnam. Mentioning them here does not suggest that any company listed in this report is involved in any of them.

  • No AD/CVD order or open investigation on vinyl, SPC or rigid core flooring from Vietnam turned up in Federal Register, USITC and ITA searches on November 16, 2026. That is a search result, not a ruling: ask your broker to run the CBP AD/CVD search for your HTS line and each supplier. Medium
  • Vietnam does face 2026 AD/CVD cases on hardwood and decorative plywood, a different product (preliminary AD determination at 91 FR 10059, March 2, 2026). Medium Federal Register
  • China's multilayered wood flooring orders name "stone and/or plastic composite" as a possible core. A plank with a real wood veneer over an SPC core is a different product from LVP and needs its own scope check. Medium USITC publication 4746
  • EAPA: CBP's Recent EAPA Actions page (last modified November 6, 2026; latest entry October 27, 2026) lists no case naming vinyl or SPC flooring. CBP has found evasion through Vietnam in other products, such as Chinese hardwood plywood declared as Vietnamese (EAPA 7252) and quartz surface products (EAPA 7783, determination August 31, 2023). High CBP Recent EAPA Actions; EAPA 7252; EAPA 7783
  • UFLPA: in 2023 CBP held LVT shipments under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, first from China and then from Vietnam, Cambodia and India. The report said CBP looked at which factory made the product and where it sourced its PVC. Medium FloorDaily via CTaSC, Aug 10, 2023

Certifications a US buyer should ask for

FloorScore

Indoor air quality certification for hard surface flooring. Per the certificate text, SCS Global Services is the only body approved by the Resilient Floor Covering Institute to issue it, and certified products are listed in the SCS Green Products Guide. On any certificate a factory sends, check four things:

  • The legal name and address match the factory on your proforma invoice and on the bill of lading.
  • Your product line and thickness are named. AMY's certificate, for example, names SPC up to 8 mm and SPC with IXPE pad up to 10 mm.
  • The validity dates cover your ship date. Certificates run for a set period; AMY's ends December 31, 2026.
  • The registration number appears in the SCS guide.

TSCA Title VI (formaldehyde)

EPA's formaldehyde standard covers hardwood plywood, medium-density fiberboard and particleboard, and finished goods that contain them. A stone-plastic SPC core is not one of those panels. It applies when the product has a wood-based core or layer, as laminate does. Ask the factory to state in writing which case applies to your item. EPA

Before the first purchase order

  1. Paperwork names one company. Business registration, proforma invoice, commercial invoice, bill of lading shipper and certificates should carry the same legal name and factory address.
  2. Where each step happens. Core extrusion, decor film, wear layer, UV coat, click profiling and packing. Origin follows where the product is made, not the port it leaves from.
  3. Raw material trail. PVC resin and stabilizer suppliers with invoices, so you can answer a UFLPA request if a container is held.
  4. Spec on the PO. Total thickness, wear layer in mil, core type, plank size, click system and its licensor, carton weight. Flooring cartons usually fill a container by weight before volume.
  5. Proof before balance payment. Third-party test reports for the spec on the PO and a pre-shipment inspection on the first lot.
  6. References. Two US importers you can call. The public bill of lading records cited above name some consignees.
  7. Duty clause. Who carries a change in Section 301 rates between the PO date and the entry date.
